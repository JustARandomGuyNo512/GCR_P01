Barrel aim and tethered reticle
================================
Portable description of the first-person system in this mod.
Use it to rebuild the same behavior in another gun mod. Class names below
are the current implementation; the steps are what matter.

Idea
----
The camera and the barrel are two different aim lines.

- The center dot is the camera. It stays on the screen center. It is intent.
- The hollow ring is the barrel. It is where the shot will go, ignoring drop.
- Bullets spawn at the muzzle and travel along the barrel direction.
- Do not bend far shots back toward the screen center.
- Do not add a second lag/sway/recoil spring. The viewmodel already has those.
  The ring only reads the muzzle bone.

HUD
---
Three layers, drawn while a gun is held in first person:

1. Center dot. Solid white. Screen center.
2. Two short horizontal bars beside the dot. Their gap grows with the gun's
   spread. They stay on the camera, not on the ring.
3. Hollow ring. Solid white. Projected from the barrel ray.
   Amber (0xFFCC66) when the barrel hits something the camera ray does not
   (a wall edge, a player, anything between the muzzle and the camera target).

Hide all three when a screen is open, the HUD is hidden, the camera is not
first person, or the gun is aimed (aim progress above about 0.45). That
includes iron sights, red dots, and scopes. Bullets still use the barrel
while the HUD is hidden.

Size, at the values tuned in game (GUI scale 3):

- Draw in screen pixels. Scale the GUI pose by 1 / guiScale first, then use
  the framebuffer width and height. Otherwise GUI scale inflates the mark.
- Ring radius is 3 GUI pixels, converted to screen pixels (radius * round(guiScale)).
- Ring stroke is 1 screen pixel. A pixel is on the ring when
  abs(distance from center - radius) <= 0.5.
- Clamp the ring to the screen with a margin of radius + 2. Hide it when the
  world point is behind the camera.
- Center dot is 1 GUI pixel. Each bar is 2 GUI pixels long and 1 GUI pixel tall.
  Gap is 2 GUI pixels plus spread * 6. Spread here is the same degrees value
  the server uses for (gaussian - 0.5) * spread.
- Colors are fully opaque. White is 0xFFFFFFFF. Blocked is 0xFFFFCC66.

Per-frame order
---------------
1. At the start of the local player's first-person gun draw, reset the
   "best muzzle" pick.
2. While the gun model renders, capture each eligible muzzle bone.
3. After the level is rendered, and before the HUD, solve the world ray from
   the capture, then project the hit into NDC.
4. When the vanilla crosshair layer is about to draw, cancel it and draw
   the dot, bars, and ring.
5. On fire, solve again and send origin + direction to the server.

The hand is drawn after the world stage, so the ring is one frame behind the
viewmodel. That matches a laser drawn from the same bone. Do not read the bone
outside the model render; the pose is only valid then. Drop a capture older
than 250 ms.

Muzzle capture
--------------
Bone name: MUZZLE_FLASH.
If a child module sits in the MUZZLE slot, skip the parent's bone and use the
child's MUZZLE_FLASH. Among the bones that do capture, keep the one with the
most negative view-space Z, so a suppressor tip wins over the barrel behind it.

Only capture when all of these are true:

- first person
- the entity being drawn is the local player
- the effect belongs to the gun root, not a side attachment such as a grenade launcher
- not a shadow pass
- the render context has an entity (the modify screen does not)

The bone matrix is in viewmodel space. Forward is -Z. Convert it the same way
the laser dot is converted, or the ring and the muzzle will disagree:

    fovScale = tan(fov / 2) / tan(35 degrees)
    pos = bone translation
    dir = bone rotation applied to (0, 0, -1), then normalized
    dir.x *= fovScale
    dir.y *= fovScale
    unit = irisShaderPackActive ? fovScale : 0.25 * fovScale
    pos *= unit

Iris already scales the first-person pose by 0.25, so that 0.25 must not be
applied twice. Store pos and dir. They are still camera-local.

Solve
-----
Skip unless first person, a gun is held, and a fresh capture exists.

    worldOffset = rotate localPos by the camera quaternion
    worldForward = normalize(rotate localDir by the camera quaternion)
    muzzle = cameraPosition + worldOffset
    direction = worldForward

Raycast from muzzle along direction.

- Blocks: collider shapes, no fluids, out to 192 blocks. Leaves are empty,
  same as the bullet.
- Entities: pickable, alive, not the shooter, not spectators, out to 64 blocks.
  Use the entity's pick radius. The closer of the block hit and the entity hit wins.

If something is hit, the ring sits on that point.
Compare it with a second ray from the camera along the camera look, same ranges.
The shot is blocked when the barrel hit is at least 0.4 blocks closer than the
camera hit. If the camera hits nothing, treat the camera distance as 192.

If nothing is hit, put the ring at muzzle + direction * 48 so it still shows
barrel direction and does not jump when looking at the sky.

Shots use muzzle as the origin and worldForward as the direction. Spread is
applied later, around that direction. The ring is the center of the cone.

Project
-------
Take the ring's world point relative to the camera. Transform by the model-view
matrix, then the projection matrix, saved from the level render (the same
matrices the laser uses). Iris needs the camera quaternion here; the raw
model-view matrix is wrong under a shader pack.

    if clip.w <= 0.05: behind the camera, hide the ring
    ndcX = clip.x / clip.w
    ndcY = clip.y / clip.w
    screenX = (ndcX * 0.5 + 0.5) * framebufferWidth
    screenY = (1 - (ndcY * 0.5 + 0.5)) * framebufferHeight

Fire
----
The client solves at the moment of the shot and sends:

    useBarrel   boolean
    origin      3 doubles, only if useBarrel
    direction   3 floats, only if useBarrel

The server accepts that aim only when:

- the shooter is a player
- origin and direction are finite
- origin is within 5 blocks of the eye (distance squared <= 25)
- direction length squared is between 0.25 and 4
- direction dot look direction is >= 0 (not more than 90 degrees off the look)

Otherwise spawn at the eye along look yaw/pitch plus the separate gun-kick angles.
When the barrel aim is accepted, do not add gun kick again. The viewmodel pose
already includes it, so it is already in the direction.

Spread stays server-side: (nextGaussian() - 0.5) * spread degrees of pitch and
yaw, applied by turning the direction into yaw/pitch, adding the offsets, and
turning it back into a vector. Bullet speed in this mod is 30 blocks per tick.
The ring does not show spread or projectile drop.

What another mod must supply
----------------------------
- A muzzle transform in the same space as the first-person gun, updated every
  frame the gun is drawn. A named bone is one way. Any matrix with position and
  a forward axis works.
- A client-to-server shot message that can carry an origin and a direction,
  plus the server checks above. Without the checks, a client can spawn shots
  away from the player.
- A HUD draw after the world projection matrices exist.
- The same collision rules as the projectile, or the ring will mark surfaces
  the bullet flies through.

Leave out
---------
Third-person ergonomics, a handling spring, stance recoil tables, lock-on
reticles, and drawing bullet drop into the ring. Those are not part of this
system. Third person and a missing bone fall back to the eyes and a center dot
only.
